Why Prices Differ

Leaf Volume Changes Everything

Two houses on the same street, same lot size, can be a $100 difference in leaf cleanup — and it isn't arbitrary. Here's exactly what moves the number, so you can see where your yard lands before you ever call.

🌳 Tree Cover Beats Lot Size

Lot size sets the starting price, but tree cover sets the real one. A .20-acre lot under three mature oaks produces far more leaf material than a .40-acre lot with a couple of young maples. We price the leaves, not the square footage.

💧 Wet Leaves Weigh Triple

Dry leaves blow. Wet leaves mat down, stick to the turf, and have to be raked and lifted by hand. After a rainy stretch the same yard can take close to twice the labor — this is the single biggest swing factor in fall pricing.

📅 How Long It's Been

Leaves cleared weekly stay a quick job. Leaves left for a month compress into a heavy, damp layer that has to be broken up before it can be moved. Waiting until December almost always costs more than two visits in November.

🍁 Leaf Type Matters

Oak leaves are thick, leathery, and slow to break down — they resist mulching and fill bags fast. Maple and ash shred easily. A yard full of oaks may need Tier 2 or 3 where a maple yard would be fine with Tier 1.

🏡 Beds, Slopes & Obstacles

Open lawn is fast. Leaves packed into landscape beds, along fence lines, under decks, in window wells, or on a slope have to be worked out by hand. Heavily landscaped yards take longer than their acreage suggests.

♻️ Where They End Up

Mulching costs the least because nothing leaves. Bagging adds labor and materials. Hauling adds loading, transport, and disposal fees. That's the entire reason the three tiers exist — you only pay for as far as you need it taken.

FAQ

Leaf Cleanup Questions

Straight answers on pricing, tiers, and fall scheduling.

What's the difference between the three tiers?

Tier 1 mulches leaves back into the lawn — nothing leaves the property. Tier 2 collects and bags them and stages the bags at your curb. Tier 3 does all of that and hauls the bags away entirely.

How much does leaf cleanup cost?

Mow-over runs $40 / $60 / $80 by property size. Bag & leave runs $99 / $149 / $199. Full haul-away runs $169 / $249 / $349. Anything over half an acre gets quoted after a quick look.

Why does pricing vary within a tier?

Leaf volume drives labor more than lot size does. Tree cover, leaf depth, whether they're wet or dry, and how long it's been since the last cleanup can all swing the same-size yard by an hour or more.

When should I schedule?

Mid-October through early December for most Indianapolis yards. Many customers book two visits — one after peak drop, one final pass before the hard freeze so leaves don't mat down over winter.

Do I need to supply bags?

No. We bring all the bags on Tier 2 and Tier 3. On Tier 1 there's nothing to bag — the leaves get mulched straight back into the lawn.

Can you do recurring fall cleanups?

Yes, and it's usually cheaper than one late-season rescue. Two or three visits across October and November keep leaves from ever compressing. No contracts — change or cancel any visit.
