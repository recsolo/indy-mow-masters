Why Mulch

Benefits of Fresh Mulch for Indianapolis Landscapes

Moisture Retention

Mulch slows evaporation from the soil, so roots stay moist longer and you water less — a real difference in an Indianapolis July.

Weed Prevention

A 2–3 inch layer blocks sunlight from weed seeds below, cutting germination in your beds and the weeding that comes with it.

Curb Appeal

Dark, even mulch against green plants and crisp bed edges is the single quickest upgrade to how a home looks from the street.

Soil Temperature

Mulch insulates the soil — cooler in summer, warmer in fall — protecting roots from Indiana's temperature swings.

Tip 1 · Depth

How Deep Should Mulch Be?

Two to three inches. That is deep enough to block weeds and hold moisture, and shallow enough that water and air still reach the roots.

Thinner than 2 inches and sunlight gets through, so weeds do too. Deeper than about 4 inches and the layer starts shedding water, staying soggy underneath, and smothering shallow roots.

Refreshing an old bed? Measure what's already there. If you still have 2 inches of broken-down mulch, a 1-inch top-dress restores the color without burying your plants.

The depth gauge

1 inToo thin — weeds get light
2 inGood for a refresh
3 inIdeal for new beds
4 in+Too deep — smothers roots

Types of Mulch We Install — and Which to Choose

All four are organic wood mulches that break down and feed the soil. The choice mostly comes down to the look you want and where the bed is.

Match the house, not the neighbor

Brown works with nearly everything. Black looks best against light siding and stone. Red belongs with brick. If you're unsure, ask — we'll look at the house when we quote.

Dyed mulch needs a dry day

Colorant has to cure. Give dyed mulch a day or two of dry weather before heavy rain or sprinklers, or the color can run onto concrete.

Stick with one color

Switching from red to black takes more than a top-dress, because the old color shows through as it settles. Changing colors usually means a full-depth install.

Mulching Tips

Ten Mulching Tips From the Crew

Do these and your mulch will look better, last longer, and actually help your plants.

01

Weed before you mulch

Mulch stops seeds from sprouting; it won't kill established weeds. Pull them, roots and all, or they'll push right through the new layer.

02

Cut a real edge

A clean spade-cut edge a few inches deep gives mulch somewhere to stop. It's what keeps it in the bed and off the lawn after a hard rain.

03

No mulch volcanoes

Never pile mulch against a tree trunk. Keep a flat ring 2–3 inches deep and pull it a few inches back so the root flare stays visible and dry.

04

Give stems and crowns room

Leave a small gap around shrub stems and perennial crowns. Mulch packed against them traps moisture and invites rot.

05

Keep it off the siding

Hold mulch a few inches below siding and slightly back from the foundation. It keeps moisture and insects away from the house.

06

Fluff before you add

Old mulch mats into a crust that sheds water. Rake it loose first — sometimes that alone freshens a bed enough to skip a year.

07

Skip the fabric in planted beds

Landscape fabric blocks mulch from feeding the soil, and weeds root on top of it anyway. Save it for under stone, not around plants.

08

Water first, then mulch

Mulch holds in whatever moisture is there. Laying it over bone-dry soil locks the dryness in, so water the bed or mulch after a rain.

09

Top-dress, don't bury

Year after year of full 3-inch layers adds up fast. Check the depth, add only what's needed, and keep the total around 3 inches.

10

Mind the downspouts

A downspout that dumps into a bed will wash a trench through new mulch. Extend it or add a splash block — and keep the gutters clear so they don't overflow onto the beds.

Do

  • Keep mulch 2–3 inches deep
  • Weed and edge beds first
  • Leave the tree's root flare showing
  • Rake old mulch loose before topping up
  • Let dyed mulch dry before rain

Don't

  • Pile mulch against trunks or stems
  • Stack new layers until it's 5 inches deep
  • Run mulch up against the siding
  • Lay fabric under mulch in planted beds
  • Mulch over weeds and hope

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions — Mulch Installation Indianapolis

How deep should mulch be installed?

We recommend 2–3 inches for most flower beds and landscape borders. That's deep enough to suppress weeds and retain moisture without suffocating plant roots. More than about 4 inches total can keep water and air from the roots, and we never mound mulch against tree trunks or plant crowns.

How much mulch do I need?

Multiply bed length by width for square feet. One cubic yard covers about 108 sq ft at 3 inches deep, or 162 sq ft at 2 inches, and equals 13.5 two-cubic-foot bags. The mulch calculator above does the math.

When is the best time to mulch in Indianapolis?

Mid to late spring — typically late April through May — once the soil has warmed and cleanup and edging are done. A lighter fall application also helps protect roots through winter.

How often should I replace mulch?

Most Indianapolis homeowners refresh every 1–2 years. Organic mulch breaks down and feeds the soil, which is a benefit, but it thins and fades. Often a 1-inch top-dress is all a bed needs, as long as the total stays around 3 inches.

What color mulch should I choose?

Brown hardwood blends naturally with most landscapes. Black dyed mulch creates high contrast and a modern look. Red complements brick and warm-toned stone. We can advise based on your home's exterior — just ask when you request a quote.

What is volcano mulching and why is it bad?

It's mulch piled in a cone against a tree trunk. It holds moisture on the bark, invites rot, insects, and rodents, and encourages roots to circle the trunk. Mulch around a tree should be a flat ring 2–3 inches deep, pulled back so the root flare stays visible.

Should I put landscape fabric under mulch?

In planted beds we generally advise against it. Fabric stops mulch from feeding the soil, weeds eventually root on top of it, and it makes adding or dividing plants harder. A proper 2–3 inch layer over a weeded, edged bed does the job without it.
